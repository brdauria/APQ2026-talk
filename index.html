AIMD processes

AIMD means Additive-Increase Multiplicative-Decrease:
a positive process that grows gradually and is reduced proportionally at random decrease epochs.

additive increase
$X$ grows linearly
→
random event
loss, congestion, reset
→
multiplicative decrease
$X\mapsto pX$
Pathwise intuition: upward crossings are continuous; downward crossings occur by jumps.
Timing: 60-90 seconds. Start from the qualitative mechanism. Light line: "AIMD is a process with admirable optimism and occasional humility." The audience should see the path before seeing exit transforms.

AIMD dynamics

Continuous-time model

\[ \frac{dX_t}{dt}=\frac{1}{c},\qquad T_n \le t \lt T_{n+1}, \]

\[ X(T_n)=pX(T_n-),\qquad 0\lt p\lt1. \]

A Poisson clock with rate $\lambda$ drives the decrease epochs.
Discrete-time model

\[ X_{n+1}= \begin{cases} X_n + 1/c, & \text{with prob. } (1-\delta),\\ p X_n, & \text{with prob. } \delta \end{cases} \qquad 0\lt p \lt1. \]

AIMD and TCP

TCP congestion control protocol is among classical applications:
it cautiously increases the transmission rate when transmission succeeds,
to decrease it sharply when congestion is detected.

Successful transmission

\[ W\longmapsto W+1 \]

Probe the available bandwidth by small additive steps.

Packet loss / congestion

\[ W\longmapsto p W,\qquad p\approx\frac{1}{2}. \]

React quickly by reducing the current congestion window.

The same rule balances three goals: stability, fair bandwidth sharing, and adaptation to changing network conditions.
Mention TCP only as motivation. The mathematical model abstracts away from protocol details.

Our starting point:

2023 AIMD scalar model

The study van der Hofstad et al. (2023)† looked at exit-time identities for the scalar model.

The model is specified by a triplet $(c,\lambda,p)$:

  • deterministic growth at rate $1/c$;
  • Poisson jumps with rate $\lambda$;
  • mult. decrease factor $p\in(0,1)$.

that determines the dynamics of the $X$ process:

\begin{align*} dX_t &= dt /c ,\qquad T_n \le t \lt T_{n+1},\\ X(T_n) &= p X(T_n-),\\ T_{n+1}-T_n &\sim \Exp(\lambda). \end{align*}

This talk - Markovian modulation

A finite-state Markov environment $J$ modulates all local parameters.

\[ (c_i,\lambda_i,p_i),\qquad \mQ=(q_{ij}),\qquad i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}. \]

The scalar transforms of the exit-times become matrix-valued transforms, indexed by the starting and ending environment regime.

\begin{align*} dX_t&=dt/c_{i_n}, \qquad T_n\le t\lt T_{n+1},\\ X(T_{n+1})&=p_{i_n}X(T_{n+1}-),\\ T_{n+1}-T_n &\sim \Exp(\lambda_{i_n}). \end{align*} \(J(t)=i_n\), and \(\P(i_{n+1}=j\mid i_n=i)=q_{ij}\).

Note: the skip-free/first-step strategy survives, but scalar $q$-series are replaced by non-commutative regime-indexed matrix series.

†R. van der Hofstad, S. Kapodistria, Z. Palmowski, S. Shneer. J. Appl. Probab. 60 (2023)

Objects of interest

For fixed levels $0\le b\lt a$, define

\[ \tau_{\up a}=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\ge a\},\qquad \tau_{\down b}=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\le b\}. \]

Upward one-sided transform $\mZ(w;\U{x}{a})$

\[ \ \mZ_{ij}(w;\U{x}{a})=\E_{x,i}\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})=j\big]. \]

Two-sided upward transform $\mZ(w;\UD{x}{a}{b})$

\[ \mZ_{ij}(w;\UD{x}{a}{b})=\E_{x,i}\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,\tau_{\up a}\lt\tau_{\down b},\,J(\tau_{\up a})=j\big]. \]

Throughout, matrix products encode conditioning on the regime reached at an intermediate level.
Explain that the j-index at exit is not decoration: it is what makes the Markov modulation tractable by matrix multiplication.

One jump, one regime change

While in state $i$, the AIMD parameters are

$$ (c_i,\lambda_i,p_i). $$

The level then evolves according to two simple rules.

  • Between jumps: $X$ increases with slope $1/c_i$.
  • At a jump: $X\mapsto p_iX$, and the new state is $j$ with probability $q_{ij}$.

$$ (x,i)\longmapsto (p_i x,j), \qquad \mathbb P(i\to j)=q_{ij}. $$

The current regime controls both the growth and the next decrease.
Read the picture from a single state. While we remain in state i, the process grows with slope 1/c_i and waits an exponential time with rate lambda_i. At the jump, the level is multiplied by p_i, then the environment moves to j according to the i-th row of Q. The important point is that the parameters used at the jump are those of the pre-jump state.

Some notation

Let $\vecz=(z_i)$ and $\vecgamma=(\gamma_i)$, with:

\[ z_i=c_i(\lambda_i+w),\qquad \gamma_i=\frac{c_i\lambda_i}{p_i}. \]

Diagonal and selector matrices:

\[ \mExp(\vecv)=\diag(\e^{v_i}),\qquad \mDelta(\vecv)=\diag(v_i), \]

\[ \mE_i=(\delta_{ik}\delta_{i\ell})_{k\ell}. \]

Some useful matrices:

\[ \mA(s)=\mDelta\!\left(\frac{\vecgamma}{\vecz-s}\right),\qquad \mB=-\mDelta(\vecgamma)^{-1}. \]

This slide prevents formula overload later. Make clear that divisions are componentwise.

Upward one-sided exit: $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

For $0\lt x\lt a$, upward passage is continuous, so the process must cross $x$ before $a$.

\[ \mZ(\U{0}{a})=\mZ(\U{0}{x})\,\mZ(\U{x}{a}). \]

Consequently,

\[ \boxed{\;\mZ(\U{x}{a})=\mZ(\U{0}{x})^{-1}\mZ(\U{0}{a})\;} \]

and we are left with computing \(\mZ(\U{0}{x})\) for any \(x\ge0\).

The proof is the strong Markov property at $\tau_{\up x}$, plus local invertibility of the matrix evolution.

Main ingredient

\[ \tau_{\up a}(0,J(0)) \disteq \tau_{\up x}(0,J(0)) + \tau_{\up a}(x,J(\tau_{\up x}(0,J(0))). \]

Timing: 2 minutes. This is the cleanest result. It is also the bridge from scalar scale functions to matrix scale matrices.

Strong Markov property at the hitting times

Upward exit: condition on the first jump — $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

First-step analysis, one entry at a time:

$$ \mZ_{ij}(\U{0}{a})=\underbrace{\mred{\e^{-az_i}\delta_{ij}}}_{\text{no jump before }a}+\underbrace{\displaystyle\int_0^{\mred{p_i}a}\mred{\gamma_i}\e^{-(\mred{z_i}/\mred{p_i})x}\sum_k\mred{q_{ik}}\mred{\mZ_{kj}}(\U{x}{a})\,dx\vphantom{\Bigg|_X}}_{\text{first jump occurs before }a}. $$

In matrix form:

$$ \mZ(\U{0}{a})=\mgreen{\mExp(-a\vecz)}+\mgreen{\mDelta(\vecgamma)}\int_0^\infty\mgreen{\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)}\mgreen{\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)}\mgreen{\mQ\,\mZ}(\U{x}{a})\,dx. $$

We start entrywise, fixing the initial regime i and the final regime j. The first underbrace is the contribution of paths with no jump before reaching a. Reaching a takes time c_i a, so the no-jump probability together with the Laplace discount gives exp minus a z_i. Since no jump occurs, the regime remains i, which gives delta_ij. The second underbrace is the contribution of paths for which the first jump occurs before reaching a. I use x for the level immediately after that jump. Since x equals p_i T over c_i, it ranges from zero to p_i a. After changing variable from T to x, the jump density together with the Laplace discount gives gamma_i exp minus z_i x over p_i. The factor q_ik chooses the new regime k, and Z_kj from x to a gives the continuation after the jump. Click 1. Now compare the red scalar quantities with their green matrix counterparts directly underneath. The no-jump term becomes the diagonal matrix E of minus a z. Gamma_i becomes Delta gamma. The regime-dependent upper limits p_i a become a common integral to infinity together with the diagonal indicator Delta of x less than or equal to a p. The exponential factors become E of minus z over p times x. Finally the sum over k becomes matrix multiplication by Q.

Upward exit: condition on the first jump — $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

In matrix form:

$$ \mZ(\U{0}{a})=\mExp(-a\vecz)+\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)\mQ\,\mred{\mZ}(\U{x}{a})\,dx. $$

Using the spatial factorization:

$$ \mZ(\U{0}{a})=\mExp(-a\vecz)+\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)\mQ\,\mgreen{\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{x})\mZ(\U{0}{a})}\,dx. $$

We now start from exactly the matrix equation obtained in the previous step. The only object I want to change is the continuation matrix from x to a, highlighted in red. Click 1. From the spatial factorization proved before, Z from x to a equals Z from zero to x inverse times Z from zero to a. This is the green replacement. Notice the order of the factors. In the scalar case the order would be invisible, but here it matters.

Upward exit: condition on the first jump — $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

After the spatial factorization:

$$ \mred{\mZ(\U{0}{a})}=\mExp(-a\vecz)+\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)\mQ\,\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{x})\mred{\mZ(\U{0}{a})}\,dx. $$

Multiply on the right by $\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{a})$:

$$ \mgreen{\mId}=\mExp(-a\vecz)\mgreen{\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{a})}+\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)\mQ\,\mgreen{\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{x})}\,dx. $$

The same matrix Z from zero to a now occurs on the left and at the far right inside the integral. These are the red objects. Click 1. Multiply the whole identity on the right by Z from zero to a inverse. The side matters because matrix multiplication is not commutative. The left-hand side becomes the identity. The factor at the far right inside the integral cancels. The no-jump term acquires the inverse matrix on its right. We are left with an integral equation directly for the inverse upward scale matrix. This is the equation that we will transform and solve. From now on I will use similar matrix manipulations without displaying all the intermediate algebra.

Upward scale matrix: Laplace transform — $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

We have obtained an equation for the inverse scale matrix $\mU(a)=\mZ^{-1}(\U{0}{a})$:

$$ \mId=\mExp(-a\vecz)\mU(a)+\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(x\le a\vecp)\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)x)\mQ\,\mU(x)\,dx. $$

Take the spatial Laplace transform

$$ \tmU(s)=\int_0^\infty\e^{-sa}\mU(a)\,da. $$

Reversing the order of integration and separating the regimes gives

$$ \boxed{\tmU(s)=\mA(s)\mB+\mA(s)\sum_{i=1}^m\mE_i\mQ\,\tmU(s/p_i).} $$

The integral equation becomes a self-similar recursion in the Laplace variable.
We have finished the detailed matrix manipulation in the spatial variable. The unknown is now U of a, the inverse upward scale matrix. Click 1. Take the Laplace transform with respect to a. In the integral term, reverse the order of integration. The selector matrices E_i separate the different regime-dependent factors p_i. The key point is the appearance of the rescaled arguments s over p_i. The original integral equation has therefore become a self-similar functional equation. From this point onward I will not show every intermediate matrix-algebra step.

Upward scale matrix: solving the recursion — $\mZ(\U{x}{a}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\up a}};\,J(\tau_{\up a})\big]$

Each iteration chooses a regime and rescales the Laplace argument by its factor $p_i$.

For a regime word $\alpha=(i_1,\ldots,i_n)$, define $\alpha\upord_k=(i_1,\ldots,i_k)$ and $p_\alpha=\prod_{\ell=1}^{|\alpha|}p_{i_\ell}$.

$$ \mK_\alpha(s)=\mA(s)\mE_{i_1}\mQ\prod_{k=2}^{|\alpha|}\mA\!\left(s/p_{\alpha\upord_{k-1}}\right)\mE_{i_k}\mQ. $$

Summing over all possible regime words defines the spatial resolvent

$$ (\cR F)(s)=F(s)+\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\mK_\alpha(s)F(s/p_\alpha). $$

$$ \boxed{\tmU(s)=(\cR(\mA\mB))(s).} $$

Scalar iteration index $n$ becomes a word of regimes $\alpha=(i_1,\ldots,i_n)$.
The functional equation is solved by iteration. Each iteration corresponds to choosing the regime encountered at the next jump. After n iterations we therefore have a sequence i_1 through i_n, which we encode as the word alpha. The product p_alpha records the cumulative spatial rescaling along that sequence. The factors E_i Q record the corresponding regime transitions. Click 1. Summing over every word of every length defines the spatial resolvent R. This is the matrix analogue of the scalar series that appears in the non-modulated AIMD model. Click 2. Applying this resolvent to the forcing term A B gives the solution. For m equal to one, there is only one word of each length and the construction collapses back to the scalar case. The words are finite, but the notation is doing its best to look infinite.

Comparison with the scalar benchmark

Scalar AIMD

$$ Z^\up(w;x,a)=\frac{Z^\up(w;0,a)}{Z^\up(w;0,x)} $$

$$ Z^\up(w;0,x)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/(w+\lambda))^k}{k!\,{\color{blue}(\lambda/(w+\lambda);p)_k}}\right)^{-1}, \qquad (c=1). $$

Markov-modulated AIMD

The scalar iteration index $k$ becomes a regime word $\alpha=(i_1,\ldots,i_k)$.

The geometric factor $p^k$ becomes the path-dependent product $p_\alpha=\prod_{\ell=1}^{|\alpha|}p_{i_\ell}$.

The scalar $q$-series is replaced by the non-commutative spatial resolvent $\cR$, with $\mE_i\mQ$ recording the regime selected at each jump.

Thus $k \longleftrightarrow \alpha$, $p^k \longleftrightarrow p_\alpha$, and the scalar $q$-series $\longleftrightarrow$ the matrix word series.

For $m=1$ there is only one possible word of each length, so the matrix construction reduces to the scalar formula.
From $q$-calculus: ${\color{blue}(x;q)_n}=(1-x)(1-xq)\cdots(1-xq^{n-1})$.

The main correspondence is that a scalar iteration is indexed only by its length k, whereas in the modulated model one must also remember the sequence of regimes visited. Hence k is replaced by a word alpha, p^k by p_alpha, and the scalar q-series by the non-commutative word resolvent. For m=1 there is exactly one word of each length, and the construction reduces to the scalar case.

Downward one-sided exit: first jump — $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

For $x\le b$, $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\mId$. For $x>b$, downward passage can only occur at a jump.

Condition on the first jump, with $T_i\sim\Exp(\lambda_i)$:

$$ \mZ(\D{x}{b})=\sum_{i=1}^m\mE_i\int_0^\infty\lambda_i\e^{-(w+\lambda_i)t}\,\mQ\,\mZ\!\left(\D{p_i(x+t/c_i)}{b}\right)\,dt. $$

With $y=p_i(x+t/c_i)$,

$$ \boxed{\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\mDelta(\vecgamma)\mExp(x\vecz)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(y\ge x\vecp)\,\mExp(-(\vecz/\vecp)y)\,\mQ\,\mZ(\D{y}{b})\,dy.} $$

Taking the spatial Laplace transform, exchanging the order of integration and evaluating the inner integral gives:

$$ \widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\mA(s)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(y\ge\vecp b)\Big[\mExp(-sy/\vecp)-\mExp\!\bigl(b(\vecz-s\mathbf1)-(\vecz/\vecp)y\bigr)\Big]\mQ\,\mZ(\D{y}{b})\,dy. $$

Starting above b, the deterministic motion is upward, so the first downward crossing cannot happen before the first jump. In phase i, just before the jump the level is x+t/c_i; after the jump it is p_i(x+t/c_i), and Q selects the new phase. The change of variable y=p_i(x+t/c_i) produces gamma_i and puts the equation in the same diagonal-matrix language used for the upward problem.

Downward exit: transform and reverse — $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

Set $\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\int_b^\infty\e^{-sx}\mZ(\D{x}{b})\,dx$.

Transform the first-step equation:

$$ \widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\mDelta(\vecgamma)\int_b^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty\mDelta(y\ge x\vecp)\,\mExp\!\left((\vecz-s\mathbf1)x-(\vecz/\vecp)y\right)\mQ\,\mZ(\D{y}{b})\,dy\,dx. $$

Reverse the region $x\ge b,\;y\ge x\vecp$ componentwise:

$x\ge b,\quad y\ge x\vecp$
$\Longrightarrow$
$y\ge b\vecp,\quad b\le x\le y/\vecp$

$$ \widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\mA(s)\int_0^\infty\mDelta(y\ge\vecp b)\Big[\mExp(-sy/\vecp)-\mExp\!\bigl(b(\vecz-s\mathbf1)-(\vecz/\vecp)y\bigr)\Big]\mQ\,\mZ(\D{y}{b})\,dy. $$

Known strip

For $p_i b\le y\lt b$, $\mZ(\D{y}{b})=\mId$.

This gives the explicit forcing $\mR_b(s)$.

Unknown part: $y\ge b$

Evaluating the inner $x$-integral produces

$$ \e^{-(s/p_i)y}\;\longrightarrow\;\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s/p_i), $$

$$ \e^{-(z_i/p_i)y}\;\longrightarrow\;\widetilde{\mZ}_b(z_i/p_i). $$

$\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s/p_i)$ drives the spatial recursion; the $m$ fixed values $\widetilde{\mZ}_b(z_i/p_i)$ are collected into $\mH_b$.
First click: only reverse the integration region. In phase i, x greater than or equal to b and y greater than or equal to p_i x becomes y greater than or equal to p_i b and b less than or equal to x less than or equal to y/p_i. Evaluating the x-integral produces two exponential factors. The first gives the recursive transform at s/p_i. The second gives the fixed special value at z_i/p_i. Second click: split at y=b. Below b the transform is already the identity, producing R_b. Above b remain the recursive transforms and the m special values.

Downward exit: the same spatial recursion — $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

Known boundary forcing

$\mR_b(s)$ is the explicit contribution from $p_i b\le y\lt b$.

Finite-dimensional unknown

$$ \mH_b=-\sum_{i=1}^m\e^{z_i b}\mE_i\mQ\,\widetilde{\mZ}_b(z_i/p_i). $$

With

$$ \mF_b^{\mH_b}(s)=\mR_b(s)\mQ+\e^{-bs}\mH_b, $$

the transform equation becomes

$$ \boxed{\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\mA(s)\mF_b^{\mH_b}(s)+\mA(s)\sum_{i=1}^m\mE_i\mQ\,\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s/p_i).} $$

This is the same recursion as in the upward problem, with a different forcing term. Hence

$$ \boxed{\widetilde{\mZ}_b(s)=\bigl(\cR(\mA\mF_b^{\mH_b})\bigr)(s).} $$

This is the structural payoff. Once H_b is regarded as given, everything depending on the spatial variable has exactly the same recursion as before. So we do not solve another infinite-dimensional problem: the same Green/resolvent operator R does it. What remains is only to determine H_b consistently with its defining special transform values.

Finite-dimensional closure: solving for $\mH_b$ — $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

Separate the constant and linear dependence on $\mH_b$:

$$ \mH_0(s)=\mA(s)\mR_b(s)\mQ,\qquad \mH_1(s)=\mA(s)\e^{-bs}, $$

$$ \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_b=\sum_{i=1}^m\e^{z_i b}\mE_i\mQ(\cR\mH_0)(z_i/p_i),\qquad \boldsymbol{\Pi}_b=\sum_{i=1}^m\e^{z_i b}\mE_i\mQ(\cR\mH_1)(z_i/p_i). $$

The compatibility condition is now finite-dimensional:

$$ (\mId+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_b)\mH_b=-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_b. $$

If $\mId+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_b$ is nonsingular,

$$ \boxed{\mH_b=-(\mId+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_b)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_b.} $$

By linearity of the resolvent, the map H -> C_b(H) is affine: C_b(H)=-Gamma_b-Pi_b H. Therefore the defining condition H_b=C_b(H_b) reduces to ordinary matrix linear algebra. At resonant special arguments one takes the finite limit in the original transform identity before performing this affine splitting.

Boundary forcing $\mR_b(s)$ — $\mZ(\D{x}{b})=\E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

The known strip $p_i b\le y\lt b$ gives $\mR_b(s)=\diag(R_i(s;b))$, where

$$ R_i(s;b)=\frac{p_i}{s}\bigl(\e^{-sb}-\e^{-sb/p_i}\bigr)-\frac{p_i}{z_i}\e^{(z_i-s)b}\bigl(\e^{-z_i b}-\e^{-z_i b/p_i}\bigr). $$

Use only if someone asks for the explicit forcing term.

Two-sided upward exit: build it strip by strip

Start

Compute the transform on a first strip where the exit mechanism is explicit.

Extend

With the transform is known below $a$.

On the next strip $a\le x\lt a/\bar p$, every jump lands below $a$:

$$ p_i x\lt a. $$

After the first jump, the continuation is therefore already known.

Repeat $a\mapsto a/\bar p$ to cover the whole state space.
The main idea is constructive. We first compute the transform on a strip where the answer is explicit. Then suppose that everything below a has already been computed. Look at the next strip, from a to a over p bar. If the process jumps anywhere in this strip, multiplication by p_i sends it back below a. But below a the transform is already known. Therefore the only new problem is to connect this strip to its new upper boundary. Once the strip is computed, replace a by a over p bar and repeat. The two-sided problem is therefore solved one strip at a time.

Two-sided upward exit: seed and continuation

First strip

If $b\lt a\le b/\bar p$, the transform is explicit:

$$ \boxed{\mZ(\UD{x}{a}{b})=\mExp(-(a-x)\vecz).} $$

Next strip

If the transform is known below $a$, then for $a\le x\lt a/\bar p$:

$$ \mZ(\UD{x}{a/\bar p}{b})=\mExp(-(a/\bar p-x)\vecz)+\mP(x)\mZ(\UD{a}{a/\bar p}{b}). $$

$$ \boxed{\mZ(\UD{a}{a/\bar p}{b})=(\mId-\mP(a))^{-1}\mExp(-(a/\bar p-a)\vecz).} $$

First strip explicit; every subsequent strip uses the transform already computed below it.

Here $\bar p=\max_i p_i$ and $\mP(x)$ collects the known first-jump contribution, with $$P_{ij}(x)=\gamma_i\e^{z_i x}\int_{p_i x}^{p_i a/\bar p}\sum_hq_{ih}\mZ_{hj}(w;\UD{y}{a}{b})\e^{-(z_i/p_i)y}\,dy.$$

This is the result I want you to remember. There are only two ingredients. First, an explicit seed on the initial strip. Second, a continuation step. Assume that the transform is known below a. On the next strip the first term is the contribution of reaching the new upper boundary without a jump. If a jump occurs, the process lands below a, where the continuation is already known. I collect that contribution in the matrix P of x. Its explicit expression is written only for completeness. Setting x equal to a gives a finite matrix equation, which we solve by inverting I minus P of a. The whole new strip is then known. Repeating the construction gives the transform at arbitrary levels.

Two-sided downward exit: $\mZ(\DU{x}{a}{b}) = \E_x\!\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\,\tau_{\down b}\lt\tau_{\up a},\,J(\tau_{\down b})\big]$

Define

\[ \mZ_{ij}(w;\DU{x}{a}{b})=\E_{x,i}\big[\e^{-w\tau_{\down b}};\tau_{\down b}\lt\tau_{\up a},\,J(\tau_{\down b})=j\big]. \]

\[ \boxed{\;\mZ(\DU{x}{a}{b})=\mZ(\D{x}{b})-\mZ(\UD{x}{a}{b})\mZ(\D{a}{b})\;} \]

Interpretation: to all paths that go to $b$, subtract paths that first go up to $a$ and only then later hit $b$.
This is an excellent place to pause: once upward two-sided and downward one-sided are known, downward two-sided is automatic.

Reflection at the running minimum

Start with an initial minimum $u>0$ and define

\[ V_t=u\wedge\inf_{0\le s\le t}X(s),\qquad \widetilde Y_t=X(t)-V_t, \qquad \widetilde\tau_a=\inf\{t\ge0:\widetilde Y_t\ge a\}. \]

We work with the Markov state $(\widetilde Y_t,V_t,J_t)$.

When a downward jump creates a new minimum $v$, the reflected coordinate returns to $0$ and the continuation value is $\mH_a(v)$.

V is non-increasing and changes only when a downward AIMD jump creates a strict new minimum. This is the state used in the restart construction and in the martingale proof. [Source] D'Auria and Palmowski, arXiv:2609.36269, Section 6.

Restart equation at a strict minimum

At a current minimum $v$, let

\[ \rho_v=\inf\{t>0:X(t)\lt v\}. \]

Successful excursion \[ (S_a)_{ij}(v)=\E_{v,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\tau_{v+a}^\up}; \tau_{v+a}^\up<\rho_v,\,J(\tau_{v+a}^\up)=j\right]. \]
Restart operator \[ (\mathcal{T}_a G)_{ij}(v)=\E_{v,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\rho_v} G_{J(\rho_v),j}(X(\rho_v));\,\rho_v<\tau_{v+a}^\up\right]. \]

The continuation matrix at the diagonal is the unique bounded solution

\[ \boxed{\;\mH_a=\mS_a+\mathcal{T}_a\mH_a,\qquad \mH_a=\sum_{n\ge0}\mathcal{T}_a^n\mS_a.\;} \]

Indeed $\lVert\mathcal{T}_a\rVert\le \max_i\frac{\lambda_i}{w+\lambda_i} (1-\e^{-(w+\lambda_i)c_i a})<1$.< /p>

Each $\mathcal{T}_a^n\mS_a$ term means exactly n strict minimum updates before a successful excursion of height a. The contraction gives a computable Picard iteration with a geometric error bound. [Source] D'Auria and Palmowski, arXiv:2609.36269, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.2.

Harmonic continuation between minimum updates

One Picard step is realized by a finite $\bar p$-geometric method of steps: split the strip into regions where every non-minimum jump lands in already constructed territory.

For $v\le x\le v+a$, define

\[ \begin{aligned} (F_a)_{ij}(w;x,v) &=\E_{x,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\tau_{v+a}^\up}; \tau_{v+a}^\up<\rho_v,\,J(\tau_{v+a}^\up)=j\right]\\ &\quad+\E_{x,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\rho_v} (H_a)_{J(\rho_v),j}(X(\rho_v));\,\rho_v<\tau_{v+a}^\up\right]. \end{aligned} \]

\[ \mF_a(w;v,v)=\mH_a(v),\qquad \mF_a(w;v+a,v)=\mId. \]

Extend:
$\mF_a(x,v)=\begin{cases}\mH_a(x) & \text{for } 0<x<v, \\ \mId & \text{for } x>v+a. \end{cases}$

First-step conditioning gives

\[ \mathcal A^\dagger \mF_a(\cdot,v)(x)=0, \qquad v\lt x\lt v+a. \]

The lower branch automatically selects the new-minimum value after an overshoot.

The landing level cannot be integrated out. If p_i x falls below v, the extension returns $H_a(p_i x)$, exactly the restart value at the new minimum. The finite $\bar p$-geometric regions are the constructive implementation of one Picard step. [Source] D'Auria and Palmowski, arXiv:2609.36269, Proposition 6.3 and Theorem 6.7.

Doob martingale for the reflected passage

For $v\le x\lt v+a$, define the stopped row-vector process

\[ \boxed{\;M_t=\e^{-w(t\wedge\tau_a)} \vec e_{J(t\wedge\tau_a)} \mF_a\!\left(X(t\wedge\tau_a),\,V_{t\wedge\tau_a}\right)\;} \]

Then $M_t$ is bounded and is the Doob martingale of the terminal reflected-passage variable.

Before passage

Apply the reflected first-passage identity again from the current state $(X(t),V_t,J(t))$.

At passage

Passage through the reflected level is continuous from below, so $X(\tau_a)=V_{\tau_a}+a$ and $\mF_a=\mId$.

Boundedness

The transform is sub-stochastic: $0\le\mF_a\mathbf1\le\mathbf1$, hence the stopped process is bounded.

Reflected first-passage transform

For $w,a>0$, $0 \lt u \le x$, and $V_0=u$,

\[ \boxed{\; \E_{x,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\widetilde\tau_a};\, \widetilde\tau_a<\infty,\,J(\widetilde\tau_a)=j\right] =(F_a)_{ij}(w;x,u).\;} \]

Starting from the minimum

\[ \E_{u,i}\!\left[\e^{-w\widetilde\tau_a};\, \widetilde\tau_a<\infty,\,J(\widetilde\tau_a)=j\right] =(H_a)_{ij}(u). \]

Already above the reflected level


If $x\ge u+a$, then $\widetilde\tau_a=0$ and the transform is $\delta_{ij}$.
 

A downward jump cannot increase $X-V$, so passage through level $a$ is continuous from below.
At $\widetilde\tau_a$, we have $X=V+a$ and therefore $\mF_a=\mId$. Letting $t\to\infty$ in the bounded martingale gives the transform by bounded convergence. [Source] D'Auria and Palmowski, arXiv:2609.36269, Theorem 6.4.

Take-home points

1

Upward and downward passage use the same regime-word Green kernel, while the downward forcing retains the boundary $b$.

2

Reflection is a restart problem: the continuation matrix $\mH_a(v)$ retains the absolute minimum after each overshoot at every strict new minimum.

3

The Doob martingale closes the reflected transform; Kella's martingale lineage explains why this is the natural object to look for.

Close by returning to the two renewal scales: words in space and restarts at minima. The martingale is the bridge that turns the second construction into a first-passage identity.
